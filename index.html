What is DETS?

The Deep Evolution Tracing System (DETS) is a comprehensive platform for tracking SARS-CoV-2 viral evolution, variant emergence, and global spread through high-resolution genomic epidemiology. Since SARS-CoV-2's emergence in late 2019, the virus has accumulated numerous mutations across its genome. Key variants such as Alpha, Delta, and Omicron have successively dominated global transmission, driven by changes in transmissibility, immune evasion, and virulence.

Our platform integrates time-calibrated phylogenetic trees, mutational signatures, epidemiological metadata, and structural biology insights to provide a detailed, interactive view of viral evolution. Researchers can explore clade dynamics, mutation frequencies, lineage replacement patterns, and spike protein structural changes across different geographical regions and time windows.

Highlighted Research Areas

Taxonomy: Explore major SARS-CoV-2 variants (Alpha, Beta, Delta, Omicron) with mutation profiles, structure context, and phylogenetic relationships. Viral Components: Detailed analysis of spike protein domains, RNA structure, RdRp mechanics, and viral cycle dynamics with interactive 3D models. Evolution: Mutation rate dynamics, codon usage patterns, phylogenetic networks, molecular dynamics, and pandemic-scale evolutionary analysis. Characteristics: Structural and mutational analyses including mutation rate, phase separation dynamics, codon usage, and RdRp context. Data: Download curated variant sequences, mutation tables, and alignment resources for advanced analysis. Useful Links: Handy external resources and project documentation for downstream analysis and tracking.

Viral Cycle

This provides a mechanistic view of SARS-CoV-2 entry, replication, assembly, and release. Understanding the complete viral lifecycle is essential for identifying potential intervention points and understanding how mutations in viral proteins affect different stages.

SARS-CoV-2 infection begins when spike (S) engages ACE2 and is primed by host proteases (such as TMPRSS2 or cathepsin pathways), enabling membrane fusion or endosomal entry. After uncoating, the positive-sense RNA genome is translated to produce polyproteins (pp1a/pp1ab), which are cleaved into non-structural proteins (nsps) that form the replication-transcription complex.

The replication complex, centered on nsp12 (RdRp) with cofactors nsp7/nsp8 and proofreading by nsp14, generates genomic and subgenomic RNAs. Structural proteins S, E, M, and N are then produced: N packages RNA, while S/E/M assemble in ER-Golgi intermediate compartments, followed by virion release via secretory pathways.
