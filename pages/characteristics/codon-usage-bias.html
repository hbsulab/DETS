Codon Usage Bias

Codon usage bias is observed across all amino acids in the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein. The figures below summarize the full-domain pattern and the domain-specific fingerprints for NTD, RBD, CTD, and S2.

Overview

Codon usage bias is observed across all amino acids. In general, U is preferred among 4-fold degenerate codons, followed by A except for Val. U and A are preferred over C and G respectively among 2-fold degenerate codons. Similar U,A bias is observed for the 6-fold degenerate Leu and Ser, while Arg prefers A over U exclusively.

The paired overview highlights codon usage across the full spike sequence. U and A dominate the preferred codon choices across most amino-acid classes, with domain-specific deviations summarized in the sections below.

NTD

Compared to the full range, U-preference in 2-fold and 4-fold degenerate codons slightly increases. A-preference in Glu and Gln also increases. Arg is more prone to use AGA and AGG than CGN codons.

The NTD fingerprint shows a stronger preference for U- and A-ending codons in 2-fold and 4-fold degenerate sites, with increased A usage in Glu and Gln and selective use of AGA/AGG for Arg.

RBD

U-preference is more prominent across 2-fold degenerate codons, notably His, Asp and Phe. A similar trend is observed in 4-fold degenerate Pro and Tyr. Arg selectively uses AGA and AGG with A preferred but not CGN codons. Met seldomly appears in RBD.

The RBD displays a stronger U bias in 2-fold codons and a similar trend in 4-fold codons. Arg remains preferentially encoded by AGA and AGG, while Met is comparatively rare in this domain.

CTD

UC and AG bias are less prominent in 2-fold degenerate codons except His, and in Asn the UC bias is reversed; U remains preferred among 4-fold degenerate codons, and codons are more selectively used in 6-fold degenerate codons.

The CTD pattern shows weaker UC and AG bias in 2-fold codons, except for His. U remains preferred among 4-fold degenerate codons, and 6-fold codon choices appear more selective.

S2

The codon usage fingerprint in S2 highly resembles that of the full range. Notably, the U-A bias is reversed in Pro and Tyr.

The S2 profile closely follows the full spike codon-usage signature, but Pro and Tyr show a reversed U-A bias relative to the global trend.
