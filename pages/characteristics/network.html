Landscape and Network

Network Analysis Overview

Mutational networks reveal how amino acids at different positions of the spike glycoprotein coevolve and interact during viral evolution. By analyzing epistatic interactions through network analysis, we uncover the structural mechanisms driving the emergence of new SARS-CoV-2 variants. These networks demonstrate how mutations work in concert across the protein to drive fitness gains and immune escape.

Adaptive Coevolutionary Landscapes

Through sequence-specific adaptive coevolution strength (ACS), we construct coevolutionary landscapes that unveil the general fitness landscape of the spike protein throughout different stages of the COVID-19 pandemic. Variants of high fitness sit within basins, surrounded by barriers of lower fitness lineages. The majority of mutations sampled are expected to be neutral or slightly deleterious, corresponding to smooth basin areas with limited variation. Only mutations that significantly impact functional properties hold the ability to alter viral adaptation.

From the D614G background, the virus followed two distinct evolutionary trajectories: one characterized by N501Y acquisition (leading to Alpha, Beta, and Gamma variants) and another by L452R (driving Lambda and Delta variants). The emergence of Omicron marked a dramatic shift, exhibiting a flatter and deeper landscape compared to Alpha and Delta. This expanded landscape indicates increased tolerance for mutations and broader sampling of sequence space, facilitating the emergence of multiple Omicron sub-lineages. A deeper basin can tolerate more slightly deleterious mutations, while mutations leading to only slight fitness changes in well-curvature regions can result in significant fitness alterations in regions with large curvature. As the virus evolved, expansion of sequence space and increased fitness have been noted with successive emergence of Alpha, Delta, and Omicron.
