Viral Cycle

Mechanistic view of SARS-CoV-2 entry, replication, assembly, and release.

Viral Cycle and Molecular Components

SARS-CoV-2 infection begins when spike (S) engages ACE2 and is primed by host proteases (such as TMPRSS2 or cathepsin pathways), enabling membrane fusion or endosomal entry. After uncoating, the positive-sense RNA genome is translated to produce polyproteins (pp1a/pp1ab), which are cleaved into non-structural proteins (nsps) that form the replication-transcription complex.

The replication complex, centered on nsp12 (RdRp) with cofactors nsp7/nsp8 and proofreading by nsp14, generates genomic and subgenomic RNAs. Structural proteins S, E, M, and N are then produced: N packages RNA, while S/E/M assemble in ER-Golgi intermediate compartments, followed by virion release via secretory pathways.

Spatiotemporal Spike Dynamics and Evolution

Spike evolution is shaped by both space and time: tissue compartments, host immunity, and transmission bottlenecks create shifting selection landscapes where different substitutions are favored at different stages.

At the molecular level, the RBD open/closed conformational balance and S1/S2 processing efficiency influence receptor engagement, fusion route, and antibody accessibility. Over epidemic time, recurrent substitutions in immune-facing residues reflect convergent adaptation under population immunity.
