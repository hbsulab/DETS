Spike Glycoprotein

Integrated overview of spike structure, domain-level function, and mutation-driven SARS-CoV-2 evolution.

The spike glycoprotein of SARS-CoV-2 is a trimeric type I viral fusion protein that binds the virus to the ACE2 receptor of host cells. It is composed of two major subunits, S1 and S2, which coordinate receptor engagement and membrane fusion.

The S1 region includes the N-terminal domain (NTD), receptor-binding domain (RBD), and C-terminal subdomains, while the S2 region drives fusion after receptor interaction. Because these domains govern infectivity and antigenic recognition, spike remains the primary focus for variant risk interpretation.

As shown across the evolution analyses, newly expanding lineages often carry coordinated spike substitutions, especially in immune-facing sites within RBD and nearby regions. Monitoring these substitutions together with lineage prevalence is essential for early warning and interpretation.

For temporal mutation trajectories and monthly leading mutation updates, refer to the Evolution page panels (Updates and Time Course), which provide trend context for spike-associated changes.
