Omicron (BA.4/BA.5)

Paired sublineage page for BA.4 and BA.5 comparative surveillance.

Variant Introduction

Emergence Period: January–February 2022 (BA.4/BA.5 joint emergence window) – Ongoing

Geographic Origin and Spread: BA.4 and BA.5 were identified nearly simultaneously in South Africa, with BA.5 subsequently achieving global dominance by mid-2022. Both outcompeted BA.2 globally through enhanced immune evasion, particularly against monoclonal antibodies and vaccine-induced neutralizing responses.

Population Impact: BA.4/BA.5 caused infection waves even in previously infected individuals (including those with recent BA.1/BA.2 exposure). Increased reinfection frequency relative to earlier waves. Hospitalization remained modest per infection, but high case numbers maintained healthcare pressure globally.

Immune Escape Properties: Exceptional escape from vaccine-induced and infection-induced neutralizing antibody responses. BA.5 in particular showed marked resistance to monoclonal antibody therapies (bebtelovimab, sotrovimab). Cross-neutralization with earlier Omicron sublineages is weak and often one-directional, with BA.4/BA.5 immune sera providing broader coverage than BA.2-induced immunity.

Virulence Characteristics: Severe disease burden reduced further per infection compared to BA.2, consistent with adaptation toward higher transmissibility rather than virulence. ICU admission rates remained low except in unvaccinated/immunocompromised populations.

Spike Conformational Dynamics: BA.4/BA.5 share L452R (retained from Omicron but also seen in Delta) and add new RBD mutations (e.g., F486S, L452R context) that collectively shift the RBD conformational ensemble away from well-characterized antibody epitopes. The NTD is also highly flexible, presenting a "moving target" not easily recognized by common antibodies. Consistently higher RBD-up percentage (~90%) relative to earlier variants ensures maximum ACE2 accessibility.

Defining Mutations

Spike Protein Mutations (BA.4/BA.5 Unique/Distinguishing):

  • L452R – Carried forward from earlier Omicron; RBD transmissibility marker
  • F486S – RBD immune-escape marker (BA.5 particularly); critical for monoclonal antibody escape
  • R493Q – RBD antibody-targeting position retained from BA.2
  • K444N, K444E, K444T (in rapid sublineage evolution) – Epitope-shifting RBD changes
  • N460K (emerging in BA.4.6/BA.5 sublineages) – NTD immune-escape marker

Non-Spike (RNA-Associated) Mutations:

  • nsp1:A58S – Translation antagonist variation
  • nsp3:I2230T – Papain-like protease; immune antagonist
  • nsp7:V82A – Replication complex architecture
  • N gene region changes – Nucleocapsid protein immune-relevant modifications

Spike Structure

PDB Structures: BA.4/BA.5 spike structures demonstrate the advanced immune-evasion strategy:

  • 7Z8W – BA.5 spike trimer (cryo-EM), showing exceptionally high RBD-up occupancy
  • 8EKN – BA.4 RBD-ACE2 complex with L452R/F486S binding interface
  • 8F7B – BA.5 spike in complex with monoclonal antibodies, showing broad escape mechanisms

Structural Features: F486S introduces a polar side chain in a hydrophobic core, destabilizing antibody complexes while maintaining ACE2 interaction. L452R (carried forward) continues to optimize the ACE2-binding interface through hydrophobic enhancement. The NTD achieves unprecedented conformational flexibility with multiple stabilized loop configurations, making static antibody epitopes ineffective. RBD-up state occupancy reaches ~90%, ensuring nearly all RBDs are presented for ACE2 engagement at any given moment.

Functional Implications: BA.4/BA.5 represent the apex of Omicron-era immune evasion through conformational optimization rather than high-impact point mutations. The spike is a highly dynamic, ACE2-optimized structure that evades neutralizing antibodies through constant conformational sampling. This architecture may represent a stable evolutionary peak, as further immune-escape mutations face constraints from ACE2-binding maintenance and structural integrity.
